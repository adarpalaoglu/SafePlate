Key Parameters

Choose a preset to auto-configure parameters, or select Custom to adjust manually.
Number of diary days to simulate

Longer diaries provide more data for detection:

  • Short (7-30): Quick tests, less reliable
  • Medium (30-60): Good balance
  • Long (60-90): Maximum statistical power

Recommended: 60 days for reliable detection

Probability that the trigger causes symptoms
  • Low (20-40%): Weak trigger — simulates mild intolerance
  • Medium (50-70%): Realistic trigger — simulates typical food sensitivity
  • High (80-95%): Strong trigger — simulates severe allergy (but may increase confounding)

Recommended: 70% for balanced detection

Probability of symptoms without trigger (noise)
  • Very Low (1-3%): Clean signal, easy detection
  • Low (4-6%): Realistic, good detection
  • High (8-12%): Noisy, harder to detect triggers

Recommended: 4-5% for realistic testing

Percentage of meals containing the trigger
  • Rare (10-20%): Limited data, may not detect
  • Moderate (25-40%): Balanced observations
  • Frequent (40-60%): Many observations, strong signal

Recommended: 30-35% for sufficient data

Advanced Parameters
Average number of meals per day

Uses Poisson distribution for realistic variation.

  • 2.0: Two meals/day
  • 3.0: Three meals/day (standard)
  • 4.0+: Three meals + snacks

Recommended: 3.0

Ingredient count range per meal

Controls meal complexity (fallback parameter when using real recipes).

Recommended: Min 3, Max 8 for typical meals

Hours after meal when symptoms can occur
  • Short (2-4h): Immediate reactions only
  • Medium (4-8h): Typical food reactions
  • Long (8-12h): Delayed reactions

Recommended: 6 hours

Earliest time symptoms can appear after meal
  • 0-1h: Immediate reactions possible
  • 1-2h: Typical digestive delay

Recommended: 1 hour. Must be less than How Long After a Meal to Watch for Symptoms.
